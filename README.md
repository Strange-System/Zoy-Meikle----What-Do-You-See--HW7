# The image I'm recreating

The image I am using is one I took last spring on campus, April 17th, 2026. I understand that the intention was to take a new photo, but this one is uniquely pretty I think.
The image is of a cherry blossom tree, in full blossom, yet with snow resting atop it, and lightly upon the grass beneath it as well.

Contrary to a more typical tree, this one has the vast majority of its blossoms in a generally horizontal line,
with barren branches beneath pointing up to it. Atop the blossoms, snow rests. They vary by a total "blossom range height" of 
2/5ths of the total tree height (or a 1:1.5 ratio of blossom space to barren space).
In contrast to this, and not yet accounted in my definition of the tree's height, there is a sparse bundle of blossomed branches
extending vertically with modest angles from the topmost part of the tree. I would describe their angle as no more than 30
degrees away from vertical. This sharply sticks out above the rest of the tree's mass.
As for the tree's main body, it extends outward in a sort of exponential measure, where the branches grow more distant from the center at an increasing rate.
The blossoms themselves are dense and detailed, with a kind of variety that would be hard to measure. I'm not entirely confident I could capture their majesty
and variety in the scope of the entire tree, they are complex bundles of leaves angled in different orientations with edges more pastel than their darker, fuller centers.
Capturing all of this with a random generation would be impossible, without going into the third dimension. I will capture it best I can.
